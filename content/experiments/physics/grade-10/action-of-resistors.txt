TITLE: Action of Resistors in a Circuit
SUBJECT: physics
GRADE: 10
UNIT: Current Electricity (Unit 19)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Investigate how inserting resistors of increasing values (5 Ω, 10 Ω, 20 Ω) affects circuit current and bulb brightness.

MATERIALS:
- 2.5 V torch bulb with holder
- Standard resistors (5 Ω, 10 Ω, 20 Ω)
- Two 1.5 V dry cells in battery holder
- DC Ammeter (0 - 1 A)
- Knife switch and connecting leads

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Understand electrical resistance as the opposition to the flow of electric current
- Relate current flow to resistance according to Ohm's Law (I = V / R)
- Observe the inverse relationship between resistance and bulb brightness

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Connect the 5 Ω resistor in series with the battery, ammeter, switch, and torch bulb.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: resistor_5_ohm
DROP_TARGET_ID: circuit_test_gap
RESULT_COLOR: #fef08a
RESULT_STATE: current_high_bulb_bright
WHY: Low resistance (5 Ω) permits a relatively large current to pass through the filament, producing bright illumination.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Replace the 5 Ω resistor with a 10 Ω resistor and close the switch.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: resistor_10_ohm
DROP_TARGET_ID: circuit_test_gap
RESULT_COLOR: #fde047
RESULT_STATE: current_medium_bulb_moderate
WHY: Doubling the resistance halves the current contributed through this element, decreasing filament heating and brightness.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Replace with a 20 Ω resistor and record the bulb brightness and ammeter reading.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: resistor_20_ohm
DROP_TARGET_ID: circuit_test_gap
RESULT_COLOR: #ca8a04
RESULT_STATE: current_low_bulb_dim
WHY: At 20 Ω, resistance to charge flow is highest; current drops significantly and the bulb glows very dimly.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: How does increasing the resistance in the circuit affect the brightness of the bulb and the ammeter reading?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Current decreases and bulb brightness decreases as resistance increases|Current increases and bulb brightness increases|Bulb brightness remains constant regardless of resistance|The bulb flashes on and off repeatedly
CORRECT_OPTION: Current decreases and bulb brightness decreases as resistance increases
RESULT_COLOR: #854d0e
RESULT_STATE: ohms_law_inversely_proportional
HINT: Resistance opposes charge flow. Higher ohms restrict current, so less electrical power reaches the bulb.
WHY: According to Ohm's Law (I = V / R), current is inversely proportional to circuit resistance for a constant applied voltage.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Resistors oppose the flow of electric current. As resistance increases in a closed circuit with constant voltage, current decreases and the bulb dims.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- How does a variable resistor (rheostat or potentiometer) control current in everyday appliances like volume knobs?
- If the resistor was completely removed (0 Ω short circuit across bulb), what would happen to the current?
