TITLE: Dependence of Liquid Pressure on Column Shape
SUBJECT: physics
GRADE: 10
UNIT: Hydrostatic Pressure and Applications (Unit 15)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Investigate whether hydrostatic liquid pressure depends on container shape or surface area using communicating vessels of differing geometries.

MATERIALS:
- Set of communicating vessels (5 tubes of varying shapes: straight, zigzag, bulbous, narrow, wide)
- Connecting manifold PVC base tube
- Colored water reservoir beaker
- Metric ruler

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Demonstrate that liquid in interconnected open vessels finds its own level
- Understand the hydrostatic pressure formula P = hρg
- Prove that liquid pressure is independent of cross-sectional area and container shape

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Connect the 5 differently shaped glass tubes onto the manifold base pipe.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: shaped_tubes_set
DROP_TARGET_ID: manifold_base
RESULT_COLOR: #94a3b8
RESULT_STATE: assembled_communicating_vessels
WHY: Interconnecting the tubes allows liquid to move freely between all five columns under equal atmospheric pressure.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Pour colored water into the central tube until it flows across all interconnected vessels.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: colored_water_beaker
DROP_TARGET_ID: shaped_tubes_set
RESULT_COLOR: #38bdf8
RESULT_STATE: filled_equal_water_columns
WHY: Liquid distributes evenly until hydrostatic pressures at the bottom interconnected points reach equilibrium.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Use the vertical metric ruler to measure the height of the water column in each differently shaped tube.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: metric_ruler
DROP_TARGET_ID: shaped_tubes_set
RESULT_COLOR: #0284c7
RESULT_STATE: measured_uniform_heights
WHY: Comparing column heights verifies whether geometry influences liquid surface level.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: What is observed regarding the vertical height of water in the five differently shaped columns?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: All columns have the exact same vertical liquid height|The widest tube has the highest liquid level|The zigzag tube has the lowest liquid level|The narrowest tube overflows
CORRECT_OPTION: All columns have the exact same vertical liquid height
RESULT_COLOR: #0369a1
RESULT_STATE: uniform_liquid_levels_confirmed
HINT: Hydrostatic pressure at any depth depends solely on vertical depth (h), density (ρ), and gravity (g).
WHY: Because liquid pressure P = hρg depends only on vertical depth h and fluid density ρ, liquid levels equalize regardless of vessel shape.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: In communicating vessels, liquid reaches identical vertical heights across all tubes regardless of volume or shape, confirming P = hρg.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- Why does the total volume or cross-sectional area of the tube not change the hydrostatic pressure at the bottom?
- How is this principle applied in municipal water supply distribution towers and water spirit levels?
