TITLE: Potential Difference Requirement for Current Flow
SUBJECT: physics
GRADE: 10
UNIT: Current Electricity (Unit 19)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Investigate why a potential difference across circuit terminals is required for electric current to flow by comparing three different battery cell configurations.

MATERIALS:
- Two 1.5 V dry cells
- 2.5 V torch bulb
- DC Voltmeter (0 - 5 V)
- DC Ammeter (0 - 1 A)
- Knife switch and connecting leads

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Define potential difference (voltage) as the electrical pressure that drives charge flow
- Compare opposing (+ to + or - to -) versus series aiding (+ to -) cell configurations
- Understand that current flows only when a non-zero potential difference exists across a load

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Connect both positive terminals of two identical dry cells together across the bulb (opposing config 1).
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: cell_a_positive
DROP_TARGET_ID: bulb_terminal_left
RESULT_COLOR: #94a3b8
RESULT_STATE: zero_potential_no_glow
WHY: With both positive poles connected, the potential difference across the bulb is 1.5 V - 1.5 V = 0 V; no current flows (ammeter = 0 A, bulb dark).

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Connect both negative terminals of the two cells across the bulb (opposing config 2).
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: cell_a_negative
DROP_TARGET_ID: bulb_terminal_left
RESULT_COLOR: #94a3b8
RESULT_STATE: zero_potential_negative_no_glow
WHY: Because both terminals are at identical negative electric potential, the potential difference remains 0 V and the bulb remains unlit.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Connect the positive terminal of Cell 1 to the negative terminal of Cell 2 in series across the bulb.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: cell_b_negative
DROP_TARGET_ID: cell_a_positive
RESULT_COLOR: #f59e0b
RESULT_STATE: series_3V_bright_glow
WHY: Connecting cells in series (+ to -) creates an additive potential difference of 1.5 V + 1.5 V = 3.0 V, driving current through the filament.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Under which cell connection does the torch bulb light up and show a non-zero current on the ammeter?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Only when positive of one cell connects to negative of the other (series potential difference)|When positive connects to positive|When negative connects to negative|In all three connection modes equally
CORRECT_OPTION: Only when positive of one cell connects to negative of the other (series potential difference)
RESULT_COLOR: #d97706
RESULT_STATE: potential_difference_rule_proven
HINT: Electric charges only flow from a region of higher electrical potential to lower electrical potential.
WHY: Current flow requires a net potential difference (voltage) across the circuit. Equal opposing potentials cancel out, resulting in zero net voltage.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: An electric current flows through a conductor only when there is a potential difference (voltage) between its terminals.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- What would happen to the total voltage if three 1.5 V cells were connected in series (+ to -)?
- Why does a bird sitting on a single high-voltage wire not get electrocuted?
