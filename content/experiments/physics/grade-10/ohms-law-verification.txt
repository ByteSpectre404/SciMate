TITLE: Verifying Ohm's Law
SUBJECT: physics
GRADE: 10
UNIT: Electricity
DIFFICULTY: medium
DURATION_MINUTES: 20
DESCRIPTION: Build a simple series circuit and confirm that voltage is directly proportional to current when resistance is constant.

MATERIALS:
- Battery (6V)
- Resistor (100 ohm)
- Ammeter
- Voltmeter
- Switch
- Connecting wires

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- State Ohm's Law and its formula, V = IR
- Correctly connect an ammeter in series and a voltmeter in parallel
- Determine resistance from measured voltage and current

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Connect the resistor to the positive terminal of the battery.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_connect
DRAG_ITEM_ID: resistor
DROP_TARGET_ID: battery_positive_terminal
RESULT_COLOR: #3b82f6
RESULT_STATE: wire_connected
HINT: Look for the "+" symbol on the battery.
WHY: The resistor limits current flow, which is exactly what we're about to measure.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Connect the ammeter in series, between the resistor and the switch.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_connect
DRAG_ITEM_ID: ammeter
DROP_TARGET_ID: circuit_series_point
RESULT_COLOR: #3b82f6
RESULT_STATE: ammeter_series_connected
HINT: An ammeter must carry the same current as the rest of the circuit, so it goes in series, not in parallel.
WHY: Ammeters are connected in series because they need to measure the exact current flowing through the whole circuit.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Connect the voltmeter in parallel across the resistor.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_connect
DRAG_ITEM_ID: voltmeter
DROP_TARGET_ID: resistor_parallel_points
RESULT_COLOR: #3b82f6
RESULT_STATE: voltmeter_parallel_connected
HINT: The voltmeter's two leads should touch either end of the resistor, not sit inline with the current path.
WHY: Voltmeters are connected in parallel so they measure the potential difference across the component without disturbing the current.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Close the switch to complete the circuit.
ACTION_TYPE: toggle_switch
DRAG_ITEM_ID: switch
DROP_TARGET_ID: switch
RESULT_COLOR: #22c55e
RESULT_STATE: circuit_closed_active
WHY: Closing the switch completes the circuit loop, allowing current to flow.

===STEP===
ID: step-5
INSTRUCTION: Read the voltmeter and enter the voltage in volts.
ACTION_TYPE: input_reading
EXPECTED_VALUE: 4.5-5.5
RESULT_COLOR: #22c55e
RESULT_STATE: voltmeter_active_readout
HINT: The voltmeter needle should be resting between 4 and 6 volts.
WHY: This reading gives you V, the potential difference across the resistor.

===STEP===
ID: step-6
INSTRUCTION: Read the ammeter and enter the current in amps.
ACTION_TYPE: input_reading
EXPECTED_VALUE: 0.04-0.06
RESULT_COLOR: #22c55e
RESULT_STATE: ammeter_active_readout
HINT: With a 100 ohm resistor and ~5V, expect roughly 0.05A.
WHY: This reading gives you I, the current flowing through the circuit, which you'll combine with V to find resistance.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: You've now measured both voltage and current for the same resistor. Dividing V by I gives you the resistance, confirming Ohm's Law: V = IR.
FORMULA: V = IR
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- What would happen to the ammeter reading if the resistance were doubled?
- Why would connecting the voltmeter in series (instead of parallel) give a wrong or misleading reading?
