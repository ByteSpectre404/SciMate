TITLE: Measuring Upthrust and Weight of Displaced Liquid
SUBJECT: physics
GRADE: 10
UNIT: Hydrostatic Pressure and Applications (Unit 15)
DIFFICULTY: medium
DURATION_MINUTES: 20
DESCRIPTION: Measure upthrust quantitatively using an overflow can (Eureka can) and verify that upthrust equals the weight of the displaced liquid (Archimedes' Principle).

MATERIALS:
- Eureka can (overflow vessel) with spout
- Solid metal cylinder / cube marked at half volume
- Sensitive spring balance (0 - 5 N)
- Clean dry collecting beaker
- Precision digital balance

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Quantify upthrust as U = Weight_air - Apparent_Weight_liquid
- Collect and weigh the volume of liquid displaced by submerged bodies
- Verify Archimedes' Principle: Upthrust = Weight of Displaced Fluid

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Fill the Eureka can until water overflows from the spout, then place the dry empty beaker under the spout.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: water_jug
DROP_TARGET_ID: eureka_can
RESULT_COLOR: #38bdf8
RESULT_STATE: primed_overflow_can
WHY: Priming ensures any subsequently immersed volume immediately overflows precisely into the collection beaker.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Suspend the metal cube in air from the spring balance and record its initial weight (e.g. 4.0 N).
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: metal_cube
DROP_TARGET_ID: spring_balance
RESULT_COLOR: #94a3b8
RESULT_STATE: weight_in_air_recorded
WHY: The air reading represents the true weight W_air of the solid metal body.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Lower the metal cube until it is submerged up to the half-way line and collect displaced water.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: spring_balance
DROP_TARGET_ID: eureka_can
RESULT_COLOR: #0ea5e9
RESULT_STATE: half_submerged_3_7N_overflow_0_3N
WHY: Immersing half the volume displaces a corresponding volume of water into the collecting beaker (reading drops to 3.7 N, upthrust = 0.3 N, displaced water = 0.3 N).

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Lower the cube until it is completely submerged below the water surface.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: spring_balance
DROP_TARGET_ID: eureka_can
RESULT_COLOR: #0284c7
RESULT_STATE: fully_submerged_3_4N_overflow_0_6N
WHY: Fully immersing the volume displaces maximum water (reading drops to 3.4 N, upthrust = 0.6 N, displaced water weight = 0.6 N).

===STEP===
ID: step-5
INSTRUCTION: How does the measured upthrust (W_air - W_submerged) compare with the weight of the displaced water?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Upthrust is exactly equal to the weight of displaced water|Upthrust is greater than the weight of displaced water|Upthrust is less than the weight of displaced water|There is no mathematical relationship
CORRECT_OPTION: Upthrust is exactly equal to the weight of displaced water
RESULT_COLOR: #0369a1
RESULT_STATE: archimedes_principle_verified
HINT: Calculate U = 4.0 N - 3.4 N = 0.6 N, and compare with the measured beaker weight of displaced liquid (0.6 N).
WHY: Archimedes' Principle states that when a body is wholly or partially immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upthrust equal to the weight of the fluid displaced.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: The upthrust experienced by a submerged solid is quantitatively equal to the weight of the liquid displaced by the submerged volume, proving Archimedes' Principle.
