TITLE: Demonstrating Upthrust Exerted by a Fluid
SUBJECT: physics
GRADE: 10
UNIT: Hydrostatic Pressure and Applications (Unit 15)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Demonstrate that liquids exert an upward buoyant force (upthrust) on immersed objects, reducing their apparent weight on a spring balance.

MATERIALS:
- Solid metal cylinder / block
- Calibrated spring balance (0 - 5 N)
- Beaker with water
- Retort stand and clamp

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Observe the apparent loss of weight of an object when submerged in a liquid
- Define upthrust (buoyancy) as the resultant upward force exerted by a fluid
- Distinguish between true weight in air and apparent weight in a fluid

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Suspend the metal object from the spring balance in air and record its true weight (e.g. 4.0 N).
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: metal_block
DROP_TARGET_ID: spring_balance
RESULT_COLOR: #cbd5e1
RESULT_STATE: weight_in_air_4N
WHY: The spring balance measures the gravitational pull W = mg acting downwards on the suspended metal object in air.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Apply a gentle downward hand push on the hanging object and observe the balance reading.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: downward_push_force
DROP_TARGET_ID: metal_block
RESULT_COLOR: #f59e0b
RESULT_STATE: reading_increased
WHY: Adding an external downward force increases the net tension required by the spring, increasing the reading.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Apply an upward push on the bottom of the object and observe the balance reading.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: upward_push_force
DROP_TARGET_ID: metal_block
RESULT_COLOR: #38bdf8
RESULT_STATE: reading_decreased
WHY: An upward force counteracts gravity, reducing the net downward tension on the spring balance.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Lower the metal block until it is completely submerged in the beaker of water.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: spring_balance
DROP_TARGET_ID: water_beaker
RESULT_COLOR: #0284c7
RESULT_STATE: submerged_apparent_weight_reduced
WHY: Water exerts greater pressure on the bottom of the submerged object than on the top, producing a net upward force (upthrust).

===STEP===
ID: step-5
INSTRUCTION: Why does the spring balance reading decrease when the metal block is submerged in water?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Water exerts an upward buoyant force (upthrust) on the submerged object|The mass of the metal block decreases in water|Water cools down the spring balance mechanism|Gravity stops acting on submerged objects
CORRECT_OPTION: Water exerts an upward buoyant force (upthrust) on the submerged object
RESULT_COLOR: #0369a1
RESULT_STATE: upthrust_mechanism_verified
HINT: The fluid exerts pressure in all directions; the higher pressure at greater depths creates a net upward force.
WHY: Apparent Weight = True Weight - Upthrust (W_apparent = W_air - U). The upward force of the water reduces the net tension on the spring.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: An object immersed in a liquid experiences an upward force known as upthrust (buoyancy), causing its apparent weight to be less than its weight in air.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- How does the density of the surrounding liquid affect the upthrust experienced by the object?
- What would happen to the reading if the object were immersed in denser salt water vs. pure water?
