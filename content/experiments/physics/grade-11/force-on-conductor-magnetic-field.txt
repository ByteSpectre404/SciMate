TITLE: Force on a Current-Carrying Conductor in a Magnetic Field
SUBJECT: physics
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electromagnetism & Electromagnetic Induction (Unit 13)
DIFFICULTY: medium
DURATION_MINUTES: 20
DESCRIPTION: Demonstrate the catapult motor effect (Fleming's Left-Hand Rule) by observing the mechanical force exerted on a free-rolling copper conductor placed between the poles of a U-shaped magnet.

MATERIALS:
- Powerful U-shaped horseshoe magnet (North and South poles)
- Two smooth parallel brass guide rails on a wooden base
- Lightweight rolling copper conductor rod (BC)
- DC power supply (3.0 V - 6.0 V) with reversing knife switch

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Observe the catapult force acting on a current-carrying wire inside an external magnetic field
- Apply Fleming's Left-Hand Rule: First Finger = Field (B), seCond finger = Current (I), thuMb = Motion/Force (F)
- Predict the direction of motion when either current direction or magnetic field polarity is reversed

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Place the rolling copper conductor rod across the parallel brass rails between the poles of the U-magnet.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: copper_roller_rod
DROP_TARGET_ID: brass_guide_rails
RESULT_COLOR: #cbd5e1
RESULT_STATE: roller_at_rest_in_magnetic_gap
WHY: The brass rails provide low-friction electrical contact allowing the rod to roll freely when an electromagnetic force acts on it.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Close the switch to pass current from B to C through the rod inside the downward magnetic field.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: knife_switch
DROP_TARGET_ID: dc_power_supply
RESULT_COLOR: #3b82f6
RESULT_STATE: rod_rolls_vigorously_forward
WHY: The circular magnetic field around the current-carrying rod interacts with the external field, concentrating field lines behind the rod and creating a catapult force forward (F = BIL).

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Reverse the direction of current flow through the rod using the polarity switch.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: reversing_switch
DROP_TARGET_ID: dc_power_supply
RESULT_COLOR: #ef4444
RESULT_STATE: rod_rolls_vigorously_backward
WHY: Reversing current direction (I) reverses the direction of the Lorentz force vector (F = I × B), driving the rod in the opposite direction.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Which scientific rule is used to predict the direction of force acting on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Fleming's Left-Hand Rule (thuMb = Motion, First finger = Field, seCond finger = Current)|Fleming's Right-Hand Dynamo Rule|Ohm's Law of Resistance|Archimedes' Buoyancy Rule
CORRECT_OPTION: Fleming's Left-Hand Rule (thuMb = Motion, First finger = Field, seCond finger = Current)
RESULT_COLOR: #1d4ed8
RESULT_STATE: flemings_left_hand_rule_verified
HINT: Stretch the thumb, forefinger, and second finger of the left hand mutually perpendicular to one another.
WHY: Fleming's Left-Hand Rule relates the orthogonal directions of magnetic field (B), current (I), and resulting mechanical force (F).

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: A current-carrying conductor placed perpendicular to an external magnetic field experiences a mechanical force (catapult effect) described by F = BIL and Fleming's Left-Hand Rule.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- How does increasing the current through the rod or using a stronger magnet affect the rolling acceleration of the rod?
- How is this fundamental force utilized in electric motors, galvanometers, and loudspeakers?
