TITLE: Thermal Expansion of Liquids
SUBJECT: physics
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Heat (Unit 9)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Demonstrate the thermal expansion of liquids using a glass test tube filled with colored water fitted with a narrow capillary tube, observing the initial drop and subsequent rise.

MATERIALS:
- Test tube filled to the brim with colored water
- Single-hole rubber stopper with a narrow glass capillary tube
- Vessel of hot / warm water
- Marker pen / rubber band marker

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Observe that liquids expand significantly more than solid glass containers upon heating
- Explain the temporary initial dip in liquid level due to initial glass container expansion
- Understand how thermal expansion of liquids is utilized in liquid-in-glass laboratory thermometers

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Insert the rubber stopper with glass tube into the full test tube and mark the initial liquid meniscus.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: stopper_with_glass_tube
DROP_TARGET_ID: colored_water_test_tube
RESULT_COLOR: #38bdf8
RESULT_STATE: marked_initial_level
WHY: Sealing the tube forces colored liquid up into the narrow capillary bore, magnifying small volume changes.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Lower the assembled tube into a vessel containing warm/hot water.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: colored_water_test_tube
DROP_TARGET_ID: warm_water_vessel
RESULT_COLOR: #0284c7
RESULT_STATE: slight_drop_then_rapid_rise
WHY: The glass envelope heats and expands first (causing a brief level drop), followed by rapid bulk expansion of the enclosed water which rises high up the tube.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: What happens to the liquid level in the glass tube upon prolonged immersion in hot water?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: The liquid level drops slightly at first, then rises steadily above the initial mark|The liquid level drops to the bottom and stays there|The liquid level stays completely unchanged|The liquid level overflows instantly without any delay
CORRECT_OPTION: The liquid level drops slightly at first, then rises steadily above the initial mark
RESULT_COLOR: #0369a1
RESULT_STATE: apparent_expansion_verified
HINT: The outer glass expands first, increasing internal capacity briefly; then the liquid expands much more than the glass.
WHY: Liquids have a much higher coefficient of volume expansion than solids (glass). Once heat penetrates the liquid, its expansion far exceeds that of the container.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Liquids expand when heated. Because liquids expand significantly more than glass, the net liquid column rises in the capillary tube, which forms the operating principle of mercury and alcohol thermometers.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- Why does the liquid level momentarily drop before it begins to rise?
- Why is mercury or colored alcohol preferred over water in domestic and clinical thermometers?
