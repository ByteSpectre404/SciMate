TITLE: Electromagnetic Induction with a Coil and Bar Magnet
SUBJECT: physics
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electromagnetism & Electromagnetic Induction (Unit 13)
DIFFICULTY: medium
DURATION_MINUTES: 20
DESCRIPTION: Demonstrate Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction: a changing magnetic flux linkage through a multi-turn solenoid induces an electromotive force (EMF) and current.

MATERIALS:
- Multi-turn insulated copper solenoid coil (100 turns on hollow bobbin)
- Strong permanent bar magnet (Alnico / Neodymium)
- Sensitive centre-zero galvanometer (-30 to +30 μA)
- Connecting leads

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Observe that relative motion between a magnet and a coil induces an electric current
- State Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction: induced EMF is proportional to rate of change of magnetic flux
- Apply Lenz's Law: the direction of induced current opposes the motion that produced it

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Connect the ends of the wire coil to the centre-zero galvanometer with both coil and magnet stationary.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: wire_leads
DROP_TARGET_ID: galvanometer
RESULT_COLOR: #94a3b8
RESULT_STATE: galvanometer_at_zero_no_motion
WHY: With zero relative motion (dΦ/dt = 0), magnetic flux linkage remains constant and zero EMF is induced.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Push the North pole of the bar magnet rapidly into the core of the coil.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: bar_magnet_north
DROP_TARGET_ID: solenoid_coil
RESULT_COLOR: #3b82f6
RESULT_STATE: needle_deflects_right
WHY: Increasing magnetic flux through the coil induces a momentary current that deflects the galvanometer needle to the right.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Hold the magnet completely stationary inside the coil.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: bar_magnet_north
DROP_TARGET_ID: solenoid_coil
RESULT_COLOR: #94a3b8
RESULT_STATE: needle_returns_to_zero
WHY: Even though the magnetic field inside is strong, because flux is not changing (rate of change = 0), the induced current drops instantly to zero.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Pull the North pole of the bar magnet rapidly out of the coil.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: bar_magnet_north
DROP_TARGET_ID: bench_stand
RESULT_COLOR: #ef4444
RESULT_STATE: needle_deflects_left
WHY: Decreasing magnetic flux induces an EMF in the opposite direction (Lenz's Law), deflecting the needle to the left.

===STEP===
ID: step-5
INSTRUCTION: What factor determines the magnitude of the induced electromotive force (EMF) in the coil?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: The rate of change of magnetic flux linked with the coil (faster motion produces larger deflection)|The atmospheric temperature in the room|The color of the wire insulation|The length of the connecting wires to the table
CORRECT_OPTION: The rate of change of magnetic flux linked with the coil (faster motion produces larger deflection)
RESULT_COLOR: #1d4ed8
RESULT_STATE: faradays_law_verified
HINT: Faraday's Law states E = -N · (dΦ/dt). Moving the magnet faster or using more coil turns increases the induced voltage.
WHY: The induced EMF is directly proportional to the rate at which magnetic field lines cut across the coil turns (Faraday's Law).

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: An electromotive force and electric current are induced in a closed circuit whenever there is relative motion (changing magnetic flux linkage) between a magnet and a coil (Faraday's Law).

- According to Lenz's law, why does pushing a magnet into a coil encounter an opposing repulsive magnetic force?
