TITLE: Heat Capacity Comparison of Substances and Masses
SUBJECT: physics
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Heat (Unit 9)
DIFFICULTY: medium
DURATION_MINUTES: 20
DESCRIPTION: Compare temperature rises when equal heat is supplied to equal volumes of water vs coconut oil, and single vs double volume of water (Q = mcΔθ).

MATERIALS:
- Beaker A: 100 mL water
- Beaker B: 100 mL coconut oil
- Beaker C: 200 mL water
- Three identical Bunsen burners / heating plates
- Three thermometers & stopwatch

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Define specific heat capacity (c) and heat capacity (C = mc)
- Understand the relationship Q = mcΔθ
- Explain why water has a high specific heat capacity (4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹) compared to oils and metals

===STEP===

DROP_TARGET_ID: beakers_trio
RESULT_COLOR: #ea580c
RESULT_STATE: oil_hottest_double_water_coolest
WHY: Oil has a lower specific heat capacity than water so its temperature rises much higher; double mass of water has double heat capacity so its temperature rise is halved.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Why does 100 mL of coconut oil experience a much higher temperature rise than 100 mL of water when supplied with equal heat?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Coconut oil has a lower specific heat capacity than water (requires less heat per degree rise)|Coconut oil generates its own heat|Water destroys heat energy|Oil is more dense than water
CORRECT_OPTION: Coconut oil has a lower specific heat capacity than water (requires less heat per degree rise)
RESULT_COLOR: #c2410c
RESULT_STATE: specific_heat_concept_verified
HINT: Q = mcΔθ. A lower value of 'c' means a larger temperature increase (Δθ) for the same heat input (Q).
WHY: Water has an unusually high specific heat capacity (~4200 J/kg°C) compared to coconut oil (~2000 J/kg°C), requiring over twice as much heat energy for each degree of temperature rise.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Different substances have different specific heat capacities (oil heats up faster than water), and larger masses require more heat for the same temperature rise (Q = mcΔθ).
