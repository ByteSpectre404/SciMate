TITLE: Mutual Induction and the Principle of a Transformer
SUBJECT: physics
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electromagnetism & Electromagnetic Induction (Unit 13)
DIFFICULTY: medium
DURATION_MINUTES: 20
DESCRIPTION: Demonstrate mutual induction between two adjacent coils wound on a common soft iron core: switching the primary current induces a momentary EMF in the isolated secondary coil.

MATERIALS:
- Bundle of soft iron wires (ferromagnetic core)
- Primary coil (100 turns enamelled wire) with switch and 3.0 V battery
- Secondary coil (100 turns enamelled wire) 1 cm apart on core
- Centre-zero galvanometer (-30 to +30 μA)
- Connecting leads

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Define mutual induction as the induction of EMF in a secondary circuit due to changing current in a primary circuit
- Explain the role of the soft iron core in concentrating and transferring magnetic flux
- Understand why transformers operate exclusively on alternating current (AC) and cannot function on steady direct current (DC)

WHY: When DC current is steady, magnetic flux is constant (dΦ/dt = 0), so no EMF is induced in the secondary coil.

===STEP===

RESULT_COLOR: #ef4444
RESULT_STATE: momentary_deflection_to_left
WHY: As the magnetic field collapses rapidly, secondary flux decreases, inducing a momentary EMF pulse in the opposite direction.

===STEP===

CORRECT_OPTION: An induced EMF requires a continuously changing magnetic field (dΦ/dt ≠ 0), which only alternating current provides
RESULT_COLOR: #1d4ed8

HINT: Steady DC creates a static magnetic field, which cannot induce any secondary voltage without continuous change.
WHY: Mutual induction and transformers operate solely when magnetic flux is continuously expanding and collapsing, which requires alternating current (AC).

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: A changing current in the primary coil generates a dynamic magnetic flux in the iron core that induces an alternating EMF in the secondary coil, demonstrating mutual induction and the operating principle of electrical transformers.
