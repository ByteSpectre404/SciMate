TITLE: Current Amplification by a Transistor
SUBJECT: physics
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electronics (Unit 11)
DIFFICULTY: medium
DURATION_MINUTES: 20
DESCRIPTION: Demonstrate that a tiny current flowing through the base of an NPN transistor controls and amplifies a much larger collector current (Current Amplification: Ic = β × Ib).

MATERIALS:
- 2SD400 (or similar NPN) silicon transistor
- Two 2.5 V torch bulbs (L1 input base bulb, L2 output collector bulb)
- 10 kΩ variable potentiometer (volume controller)
- Dual DC battery packs (3.0 V input, 4.5 V output)
- Switches S1 and S2 & breadboard

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Identify the three terminals of an NPN bipolar junction transistor (Emitter, Base, Collector)
- Understand base current (Ib) and collector current (Ic) relationships
- Demonstrate that a small base current controls a significantly larger collector current

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Assemble the input base circuit (S1, potentiometer, bulb L1) and output collector circuit (S2, bulb L2) via the NPN transistor.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: npn_transistor
DROP_TARGET_ID: breadboard_circuit
RESULT_COLOR: #3b82f6
RESULT_STATE: dual_circuit_assembled
WHY: The base-emitter junction acts as the input control path, while the collector-emitter path carries the main power current.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Turn on switch S2 (collector output) while keeping switch S1 (base input) OFF.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: switch_s2
DROP_TARGET_ID: breadboard_circuit
RESULT_COLOR: #94a3b8
RESULT_STATE: bulb_l2_off_zero_collector_current
WHY: With zero base current (Ib = 0), the transistor is in the cutoff state and blocks collector current (L2 remains dark).

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Turn on switch S1 and adjust the potentiometer so a faint tiny current flows through input bulb L1.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: switch_s1
DROP_TARGET_ID: breadboard_circuit
RESULT_COLOR: #fef08a
RESULT_STATE: bulb_l1_dim_bulb_l2_bright
WHY: A tiny base current (Ib ~ a few mA) injects charge carriers into the base, enabling a much larger collector current (Ic ~ hundreds of mA) to illuminate output bulb L2 brilliantly.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: What happens to the large output collector current (Ic) when the small base current (Ib) is interrupted?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Collector current (Ic) drops immediately to zero, extinguishing the output bulb|Collector current becomes twice as bright|The battery short circuits|The transistor changes into a diode
CORRECT_OPTION: Collector current (Ic) drops immediately to zero, extinguishing the output bulb
RESULT_COLOR: #ca8a04
RESULT_STATE: current_amplification_verified
HINT: The base current acts as the 'valve' that opens or closes the main collector current conduit.
WHY: Collector current is proportional to base current (Ic = β · Ib, where β is current gain). When Ib = 0, Ic drops to zero immediately.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: An NPN transistor acts as a current amplifier: a small input current at the base terminal controls and drives a substantially larger output current through the collector-emitter circuit.
