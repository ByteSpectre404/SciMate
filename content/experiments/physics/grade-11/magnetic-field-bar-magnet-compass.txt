TITLE: Magnetic Field of a Bar Magnet with a Plotting Compass
SUBJECT: physics
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electromagnetism & Electromagnetic Induction (Unit 13)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Investigate the invisible magnetic field surrounding a permanent bar magnet by testing deflections of a magnetic plotting compass near magnetic and non-magnetic materials.

MATERIALS:
- Permanent bar magnet (North and South poles marked)
- Magnetic plotting compass needle
- Test objects: unmagnetized iron bar, brass rod, plastic ruler, glass sheet
- Plain drawing sheet

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Define a magnetic field as the region around a magnet where magnetic forces are exerted
- Use a plotting compass to map magnetic field lines exiting North and entering South
- Distinguish between magnetic materials (ferromagnetic: iron/steel) and non-magnetic materials (brass, glass, plastic)

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Place the plotting compass on the table and note its natural North-South resting orientation.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: plotting_compass
DROP_TARGET_ID: drawing_sheet
RESULT_COLOR: #38bdf8
RESULT_STATE: compass_pointing_geographic_north
WHY: The lightweight magnetized needle aligns naturally with Earth's horizontal geomagnetic field lines.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Bring non-magnetic objects (glass, plastic, brass) near the compass one by one.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: brass_rod
DROP_TARGET_ID: plotting_compass
RESULT_COLOR: #94a3b8
RESULT_STATE: zero_deflection_non_magnetic
WHY: Non-magnetic materials lack unpaired electron spin alignment and do not exert any magnetic force on the compass needle.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Bring the North pole of the bar magnet close to the compass needle.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: bar_magnet_north
DROP_TARGET_ID: plotting_compass
RESULT_COLOR: #ef4444
RESULT_STATE: strong_compass_deflection
WHY: Like magnetic poles repel and opposite poles attract; the North pole of the bar magnet strongly repels the North tip and attracts the South tip.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Move the compass along curved paths from the North pole to the South pole of the magnet.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: plotting_compass
DROP_TARGET_ID: bar_magnet_assembly
RESULT_COLOR: #3b82f6
RESULT_STATE: magnetic_field_lines_mapped
WHY: The compass needle aligns tangentially to the magnetic flux lines at every point, tracing continuous closed loops from North to South externally.

===STEP===
ID: step-5
INSTRUCTION: In which direction do external magnetic field lines travel outside a permanent bar magnet?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: From North pole to South pole in continuous closed curves|From South pole to North pole|Radially outward in all directions indefinitely|In random chaotic zigzags
CORRECT_OPTION: From North pole to South pole in continuous closed curves
RESULT_COLOR: #1d4ed8
RESULT_STATE: field_direction_verified
HINT: By scientific convention, magnetic field lines emerge from the North pole and enter into the South pole.
WHY: External magnetic field lines represent the direction of force that would act on an isolated magnetic North pole, pointing from North to South.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: A permanent magnet creates a vector magnetic field around itself. Field lines exit the North pole, curve through space, and enter the South pole, exerting forces on magnetic materials and compasses.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- Why do two magnetic field lines never cross or intersect each other?
- How does Earth behave like a giant bar magnet with its magnetic South pole near the geographic North Pole?
