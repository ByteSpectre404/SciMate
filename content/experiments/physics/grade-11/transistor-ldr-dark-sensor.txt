TITLE: Light-Sensitive Automatic Switch (LDR Dark Sensor)
SUBJECT: physics
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electronics (Unit 11)
DIFFICULTY: medium
DURATION_MINUTES: 20
DESCRIPTION: Construct an automatic night light circuit using an NPN transistor, Light Dependent Resistor (LDR), and potential divider that automatically illuminates a lamp when darkness falls.

MATERIALS:
- NPN Transistor (D400 or D313)
- Light Dependent Resistor (LDR)
- 10 kΩ variable potentiometer (sensitivity control)
- 2.5 V torch bulb / LED
- 3.0 V DC battery & circuit board
- Opaque cover / fingertip

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Understand that LDR resistance decreases in bright light and increases drastically in darkness
- Assemble a potential divider circuit to feed the base of an NPN transistor
- Explain how automatic streetlights and darkness-activated security lighting operate

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Assemble the potential divider with the LDR and 10 kΩ potentiometer connected to the base of the NPN transistor.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: ldr_component
DROP_TARGET_ID: breadboard_circuit
RESULT_COLOR: #3b82f6
RESULT_STATE: light_sensor_circuit_ready
WHY: The potential divider splits the supply voltage so that base voltage Vbe is controlled by the ratio of LDR resistance to potentiometer resistance.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Expose the LDR to ambient room light and observe the indicator bulb.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: ambient_light_source
DROP_TARGET_ID: ldr_component
RESULT_COLOR: #94a3b8
RESULT_STATE: bright_light_bulb_off
WHY: In light, LDR resistance is low (a few hundred ohms), pulling base voltage below 0.7 V so the transistor remains OFF.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Cover the LDR with an opaque cap / fingertip to simulate total darkness.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: opaque_cap
DROP_TARGET_ID: ldr_component
RESULT_COLOR: #fef08a
RESULT_STATE: darkness_bulb_turns_on_brightly
WHY: In darkness, LDR resistance surges to hundreds of kilo-ohms, pushing Vbe above the 0.7 V threshold and switching the transistor ON to illuminate the lamp.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Why does the lamp turn ON automatically when the LDR is placed in darkness?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: In darkness, LDR resistance increases greatly, raising the transistor base voltage above 0.7 V to turn it ON|The darkness generates new electric charges inside the wires|LDR generates electricity when covered|The bulb senses heat from the finger
CORRECT_OPTION: In darkness, LDR resistance increases greatly, raising the transistor base voltage above 0.7 V to turn it ON
RESULT_COLOR: #ca8a04
RESULT_STATE: automatic_switch_principle_verified
HINT: LDR resistance is inversely related to light intensity. High resistance in darkness increases voltage across the bottom arm of the potential divider.
WHY: An LDR has high resistance in the dark. In this potential divider network, this increases base voltage Vbe beyond 0.7 V, driving the transistor into saturation (ON).

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Combining an LDR with a transistor potential divider creates an automatic darkness sensor that switches the lamp ON in the dark and OFF in the light, forming the basis of automated street lighting.
