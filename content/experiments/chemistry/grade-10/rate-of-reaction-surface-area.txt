TITLE: Effect of Surface Area of Reactants on Rate of Reaction
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 10
UNIT: Rate of Reactions (Unit 17)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Compare the rate of reaction between dilute hydrochloric acid and equal masses of solid calcium carbonate chips versus finely ground calcium carbonate powder.

MATERIALS:
- Large calcium carbonate chips (CaCO3, 2.0 g)
- Fine calcium carbonate powder (CaCO3, 2.0 g)
- Dilute hydrochloric acid (HCl, 1.0 M, 50 mL in two beakers)
- Stopwatch & digital balance

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Explain collision theory in terms of surface area and frequency of effective collisions
- Observe faster effervescence and shorter dissolution time for powdered reactants
- Formulate the reaction: CaCO3(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl2(aq) + H2O(l) + CO2(g)

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Add 2.0 g of large CaCO3 chips into Beaker 1 containing 50 mL dilute HCl and start the stopwatch.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: caco3_chips
DROP_TARGET_ID: acid_beaker_1
RESULT_COLOR: #f1f5f9
RESULT_STATE: slow_steady_effervescence
WHY: In large solid chips, only the outer surface particles are accessible for collision with H+ ions, leading to a slow reaction rate.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Add 2.0 g of fine CaCO3 powder into Beaker 2 containing 50 mL dilute HCl and start timing.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: caco3_powder
DROP_TARGET_ID: acid_beaker_2
RESULT_COLOR: #e2e8f0
RESULT_STATE: vigorous_effervescence_fast_dissolution
WHY: Grinding into fine powder drastically increases total exposed surface area, increasing the frequency of collisions per second between reactants.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: How does decreasing particle size (increasing surface area) affect the rate of reaction?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Increasing surface area increases the rate of reaction (powder reacts much faster than chips)|Surface area has no effect on reaction rate|Larger chips react faster because they are heavier|Powder slows down the reaction rate
CORRECT_OPTION: Increasing surface area increases the rate of reaction (powder reacts much faster than chips)
RESULT_COLOR: #0284c7
RESULT_STATE: surface_area_effect_verified
HINT: More exposed surface allows more reactant collisions to happen simultaneously in every second.
WHY: According to collision theory, increasing surface area exposes more reactant particles to collisions, increasing collision frequency and the reaction rate.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Finely powdered calcium carbonate reacts much faster with dilute hydrochloric acid than large chips because the greater surface area allows a higher frequency of successful collisions.
