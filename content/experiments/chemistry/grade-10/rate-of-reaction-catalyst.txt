TITLE: Effect of a Catalyst on Rate of Chemical Reaction
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 10
UNIT: Rate of Reactions (Unit 17)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Investigate how manganese dioxide (MnO2) acts as a catalyst to accelerate the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) without being chemically consumed.

MATERIALS:
- Fresh hydrogen peroxide solution (H2O2, 6% / 20 vol)
- Manganese dioxide black powder (MnO2, 0.2 g)
- Two test tubes & test tube rack
- Filter paper, funnel, evaporating dish & balance
- Glowing wooden splint

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Define a catalyst as a substance that increases the rate of reaction without being consumed
- Explain how catalysts provide an alternative reaction pathway with lower activation energy
- Demonstrate that catalyst mass is conserved after filtration and drying (2H2O2 → 2H2O + O2)

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Add 10 mL of fresh hydrogen peroxide solution into two test tubes (Tube A and Tube B).
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: h2o2_solution
DROP_TARGET_ID: test_tubes_pair
RESULT_COLOR: #f8fafc
RESULT_STATE: slow_natural_decomposition
WHY: Pure H2O2 decomposes very slowly at room temperature, releasing almost undetectable bubbles of oxygen.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Add 0.2 g of black manganese dioxide (MnO2) powder into Tube B and observe immediately.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: mno2_powder
DROP_TARGET_ID: test_tube_b
RESULT_COLOR: #1e293b
RESULT_STATE: vigorous_effervescence_oxygen_froth
WHY: MnO2 acts as a catalyst, providing a low activation energy pathway that causes rapid decomposition: 2H2O2(aq) → 2H2O(l) + O2(g)↑.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Test the effervescing gas evolved from Tube B with a glowing wooden splint.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: glowing_splint
DROP_TARGET_ID: test_tube_b
RESULT_COLOR: #f59e0b
RESULT_STATE: splint_relights_brightly
WHY: The rapid stream of evolved oxygen gas rekindles the glowing splint with a bright flame.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Filter the black MnO2 residue from Tube B, wash with water, dry thoroughly, and weigh on the balance.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: filter_funnel_setup
DROP_TARGET_ID: digital_balance
RESULT_COLOR: #334155
RESULT_STATE: mass_unchanged_0_2g
WHY: Because a catalyst participates without being consumed in the net reaction, exactly 0.2 g of MnO2 is recovered.

===STEP===
ID: step-5
INSTRUCTION: What defines the function and characteristics of a chemical catalyst?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Increases the rate of reaction by lowering activation energy and remains unchanged in mass at the end|Increases the final yield of products by reacting chemically|Gets completely consumed during the reaction|Changes an endothermic reaction into an exothermic one
CORRECT_OPTION: Increases the rate of reaction by lowering activation energy and remains unchanged in mass at the end
RESULT_COLOR: #0f172a
RESULT_STATE: catalyst_definition_verified
HINT: Catalysts accelerate reaction velocity, but their chemical identity and mass are conserved after the reaction.
WHY: Catalysts offer an alternative pathway with lower activation energy (Ea), drastically increasing reaction speed while remaining chemically and quantitatively unchanged.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Manganese dioxide acts as a catalyst for the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide (2H2O2 → 2H2O + O2), dramatically accelerating oxygen gas evolution while retaining its original mass of 0.2 g.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- How do biological catalysts (enzymes like catalase and amylase) compare to inorganic catalysts like MnO2?
- Can a catalyst initiate a reaction that is thermodynamically impossible on its own?
