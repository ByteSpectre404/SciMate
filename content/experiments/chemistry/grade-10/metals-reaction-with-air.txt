TITLE: Investigating How Metals React with Air
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 10
UNIT: Changes in Matter (Unit 16)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Compare the reactivity of alkali metal sodium (Na) and alkaline earth metal magnesium (Mg) when freshly cut and exposed to atmospheric air.

MATERIALS:
- Freshly cut sodium metal piece (handled with dry forceps)
- Cleaned magnesium ribbon strip (2 cm)
- Ceramic tile & filter paper
- Retort stand & magnifying lens

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Compare the oxidation rates of Group 1 (alkali) and Group 2 metals in atmospheric air
- Understand tarnish formation due to metal oxides, hydroxides, and carbonates
- Explain why sodium is stored under kerosene / paraffin oil

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Cut a small piece of sodium on filter paper and observe its shiny cut surface exposed to air.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: sodium_knife
DROP_TARGET_ID: sodium_metal
RESULT_COLOR: #94a3b8
RESULT_STATE: rapid_tarnish_white_crust
WHY: Highly reactive sodium reacts rapidly with oxygen and atmospheric moisture, losing its silvery luster within seconds to form Na2O, NaOH, and Na2CO3.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Clean a 2 cm magnesium ribbon with sandpaper to expose shiny metal and leave exposed to air.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: sandpaper
DROP_TARGET_ID: magnesium_ribbon
RESULT_COLOR: #cbd5e1
RESULT_STATE: slow_thin_protective_oxide_layer
WHY: Magnesium reacts very slowly at room temperature, forming a tough, thin, protective coating of magnesium oxide (MgO) that passivates the surface.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: How does the speed of reaction with atmospheric air compare between sodium and magnesium?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Sodium tarnishes immediately while magnesium tarnishes very slowly at room temperature|Magnesium reacts faster than sodium|Both metals catch fire instantly in room air|Neither metal reacts with oxygen or air
CORRECT_OPTION: Sodium tarnishes immediately while magnesium tarnishes very slowly at room temperature
RESULT_COLOR: #64748b
RESULT_STATE: reactivity_comparison_verified
HINT: Sodium belongs to Group 1 with a single loosely held valence electron, making it far more reactive than Group 2 magnesium.
WHY: Sodium has a much lower ionization energy and higher chemical reactivity than magnesium, reacting vigorously with atmospheric air at room temperature.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Sodium reacts rapidly with atmospheric oxygen and water vapor to form a dull white oxide-hydroxide crust, while magnesium reacts very slowly at room temperature.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- Why must sodium metal be preserved under liquid paraffin or kerosene in the school laboratory?
- What happens when magnesium is heated in air compared to leaving it at room temperature?
