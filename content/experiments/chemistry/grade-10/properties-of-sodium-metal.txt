TITLE: Observation of the Properties of Sodium Metal
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 10
UNIT: Elements and Compounds
DIFFICULTY: medium
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Examine the softness and high reactivity of alkali metal sodium with oxygen and moisture when exposed to air.

MATERIALS:
- Sodium metal sample (in kerosene)
- Forceps
- Lab knife
- Dry glass surface
- Kerosene oil container

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Recognize physical properties of Group 1 alkali metals (softness, luster)
- Understand why sodium is stored under paraffin oil / kerosene due to atmospheric oxidation

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Use forceps to take a small piece of sodium from the paraffin oil bottle and place on a dry tile.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: sodium_metal
DROP_TARGET_ID: dry_tile
RESULT_COLOR: #94a3b8
RESULT_STATE: silvery_grey_metal
WHY: Forceps prevent skin moisture from reacting violently with sodium.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Cut the piece of sodium metal using a knife.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_connect
DRAG_ITEM_ID: lab_knife
DROP_TARGET_ID: sodium_metal
RESULT_COLOR: #f8fafc
RESULT_STATE: shiny_cut_surface
WHY: Sodium is very soft for a metal and can be sliced easily like cold butter.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Leave the freshly cut shiny sodium surface exposed to air for 5 minutes.
ACTION_TYPE: toggle_switch
DRAG_ITEM_ID: air_exposure_timer
DROP_TARGET_ID: sodium_metal
RESULT_COLOR: #94a3b8
RESULT_STATE: tarnished_surface
WHY: Exposing the shiny metallic surface allows reaction with oxygen and water vapor in the atmosphere.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: What happens to the shiny cut surface of sodium after exposure to air?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Becomes dull and tarnished|Grows bright green crystals|Remains shiny forever|Turns into liquid water
CORRECT_OPTION: Becomes dull and tarnished
RESULT_COLOR: #94a3b8
RESULT_STATE: tarnished_surface
HINT: Sodium reacts rapidly with oxygen forming sodium oxide coating.
WHY: Sodium rapidly reacts with O2 and H2O in air to form an oxide/hydroxide crust, dulling its metallic luster.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Sodium is a soft alkali metal that tarnishes rapidly in air due to oxidation, explaining why it must be stored under kerosene.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- Why cannot sodium metal be stored in water or open air?
- What chemical compound forms on the tarnished surface of sodium metal?
