TITLE: Reaction of Magnesium with Water
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 10
UNIT: Changes in Matter (Unit 16)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Compare the behavior of cleaned magnesium metal in cold water versus boiling hot water and test the resulting basic solution and hydrogen gas.

MATERIALS:
- Cleaned magnesium ribbon (3 cm)
- Heat-resistant beaker with water
- Bunsen burner & tripod with wire gauze
- Red litmus paper & test tube

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Observe that magnesium does not react appreciably with cold water
- Demonstrate that magnesium reacts with boiling hot water to produce magnesium hydroxide and hydrogen gas
- Formulate the chemical equation: Mg(s) + 2H2O(l) → Mg(OH)2(aq) + H2(g)

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Place a piece of cleaned magnesium ribbon into a beaker of cold water and observe for 2 minutes.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: magnesium_ribbon
DROP_TARGET_ID: cold_water_beaker
RESULT_COLOR: #e0f2fe
RESULT_STATE: no_visible_cold_reaction
WHY: Magnesium reacts negligibly with cold liquid water because an insoluble layer of Mg(OH)2 quickly covers the metal surface.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Place the beaker over a Bunsen burner flame and heat the water to boiling point.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: bunsen_burner
DROP_TARGET_ID: cold_water_beaker
RESULT_COLOR: #bae6fd
RESULT_STATE: active_gas_bubble_evolution
WHY: Thermal energy provides the activation energy: Mg(s) + 2H2O(l) → Mg(OH)2(s/aq) + H2(g)↑. Vigorous effervescence of hydrogen bubbles appears on the ribbon.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Dip a strip of red litmus paper into the resulting hot solution.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: red_litmus_paper
DROP_TARGET_ID: cold_water_beaker
RESULT_COLOR: #3b82f6
RESULT_STATE: red_litmus_turns_blue
WHY: Magnesium hydroxide Mg(OH)2 is slightly soluble in water, releasing OH- ions that make the solution mildly basic.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: What is the primary product of the reaction between magnesium ribbon and hot/boiling water?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Magnesium hydroxide solution and hydrogen gas bubbles|Magnesium oxide and oxygen gas|Magnesium chloride and steam|No reaction occurs even at boiling temperatures
CORRECT_OPTION: Magnesium hydroxide solution and hydrogen gas bubbles
RESULT_COLOR: #1d4ed8
RESULT_STATE: reaction_products_confirmed
HINT: Hot water reacts with magnesium to liberate a flammable gas and form a basic hydroxide solution that turns red litmus blue.
WHY: The reaction Mg(s) + 2H2O(l) → Mg(OH)2(aq) + H2(g) produces basic magnesium hydroxide and hydrogen gas.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Magnesium does not react noticeably with cold water, but reacts with hot/boiling water to produce magnesium hydroxide (a weak base that turns red litmus blue) and hydrogen gas.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- How does the reaction of sodium with water differ from magnesium with water?
- What would happen if magnesium was reacted with steam instead of boiling water?
