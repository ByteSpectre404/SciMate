TITLE: Effect of Concentration on Rate of Reaction
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 10
UNIT: Rate of Reactions (Unit 17)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Investigate how the concentration of dilute hydrochloric acid affects the speed of reaction and rate of hydrogen gas bubbling with magnesium ribbon.

MATERIALS:
- Cleaned magnesium ribbon strips of equal length (2 cm each)
- Three test tubes labeled 1 (Low conc), 2 (Medium conc), 3 (High conc)
- Dilute hydrochloric acid (HCl) & distilled water
- Test tube rack & stopwatch

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Understand that concentration represents the number of reacting particles per unit volume
- Explain why higher concentration leads to higher collision frequency and faster reaction rate
- Compare fizzing intensity: 2.5 mL HCl (low) vs 5.0 mL HCl (med) vs 7.5 mL HCl (high) in constant total volumes

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Prepare three tubes with different concentrations of HCl (diluted to equal 15 mL total volume with water).
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: hcl_acid_bottle
DROP_TARGET_ID: concentration_tubes_set
RESULT_COLOR: #f0fdf4
RESULT_STATE: prepared_concentration_gradients
WHY: Constant total volume ensures that varying the amount of acid directly varies the concentration of H+ reactant ions.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Add a 2 cm cleaned magnesium ribbon to Tube 1 (lowest concentration: 2.5 mL acid + 12.5 mL water).
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: magnesium_strip_1
DROP_TARGET_ID: tube_1_low
RESULT_COLOR: #dcfce7
RESULT_STATE: slow_effervescence_low_conc
WHY: Lower density of H+ ions per unit volume results in fewer collisions per second with the metal surface.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Add identical magnesium ribbons to Tube 2 (5.0 mL acid) and Tube 3 (7.5 mL acid - highest concentration).
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: magnesium_strip_3
DROP_TARGET_ID: tube_3_high
RESULT_COLOR: #86efac
RESULT_STATE: rapid_violent_fizzing_high_conc
WHY: At higher concentration, more H+ ions crowd the unit volume, producing a much higher collision rate and rapid gas liberation.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: What is the relationship between the concentration of reactants and the rate of a chemical reaction?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Rate of reaction increases as the concentration of reactants increases|Concentration does not affect the rate of reaction|Lower concentration produces a faster reaction|Gas bubbles stop completely at higher concentrations
CORRECT_OPTION: Rate of reaction increases as the concentration of reactants increases
RESULT_COLOR: #16a34a
RESULT_STATE: concentration_rate_law_verified
HINT: More reacting particles in the same volume collide more frequently with the solid magnesium surface.
WHY: Higher concentration increases the particle density per unit volume, which increases collision frequency per unit time, speeding up the reaction rate.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Increasing the concentration of hydrochloric acid increases the rate of reaction with magnesium ribbon due to a higher frequency of reactant collisions per unit time.
