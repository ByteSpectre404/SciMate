TITLE: Effect of Contact with Other Metals on Iron Corrosion (Sacrificial Protection)
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electrochemistry (Unit 12)
DIFFICULTY: hard
DURATION_MINUTES: 25
DESCRIPTION: Investigate sacrificial protection versus accelerated galvanic corrosion of iron in contact with Mg, Zn, Cu, and Pb using agar jelly containing ferroxyl indicator (phenolphthalein & potassium ferricyanide).

MATERIALS:
- Five cleaned iron nails
- Strips/ribbons of Magnesium (Mg), Zinc (Zn), Copper (Cu), and Lead (Pb)
- Five Petri dishes
- Warm agar gel medium containing NaCl, Phenolphthalein (turns pink with OH⁻), and Potassium ferricyanide (turns Prussian blue with Fe²⁺)

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Interpret ferroxyl indicator color changes (Prussian blue indicates Fe²⁺ / iron oxidation; Pink indicates OH⁻ / cathodic protection)
- Demonstrate sacrificial (cathodic) protection: more reactive metals (Mg, Zn) oxidize to protect iron
- Observe accelerated galvanic corrosion: less reactive metals (Cu, Pb) force iron to become the anode and corrode faster

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: In 5 Petri dishes, arrange: Dish 1 (Plain nail), Dish 2 (Nail + Mg), Dish 3 (Nail + Zn), Dish 4 (Nail + Cu), Dish 5 (Nail + Pb).
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: metals_wrapped_nails_set
DROP_TARGET_ID: petri_dishes_tray
RESULT_COLOR: #cbd5e1
RESULT_STATE: assembled_bimetallic_couples
WHY: Direct physical contact creates bimetallic galvanic couples where the more electropositive metal becomes the anode.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Pour warm molten agar gel containing ferroxyl indicator (NaCl, phenolphthalein, potassium ferricyanide) over all dishes and let set.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: warm_ferroxyl_agar_gel
DROP_TARGET_ID: petri_dishes_tray
RESULT_COLOR: #fef08a
RESULT_STATE: gelled_indicator_medium
WHY: Agar immobilizes ion diffusion, allowing local color reactions to appear exactly around the anodic (blue Fe²⁺) or cathodic (pink OH⁻) sites.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Examine the color rings around the nails after 1 hour: Pink around Mg and Zn (protected); Deep Blue around Cu and Pb (corroding).
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: observation_timer
DROP_TARGET_ID: petri_dishes_tray
RESULT_COLOR: #ec4899
RESULT_STATE: pink_for_mg_zn_blue_for_cu_pb
WHY: Mg and Zn are more reactive than Fe, acting as sacrificial anodes (iron becomes the cathode and generates OH⁻ → pink). Cu and Pb are less reactive, forcing iron to be the anode (Fe → Fe²⁺ → Prussian blue).

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Why do magnesium and zinc protect iron from rusting when in electrical contact?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: They are more reactive than iron, so they oxidize preferentially (sacrificial protection), making iron the protected cathode|They absorb all light in the petri dish|They release copper into the agar|They freeze the water molecules
CORRECT_OPTION: They are more reactive than iron, so they oxidize preferentially (sacrificial protection), making iron the protected cathode
RESULT_COLOR: #be185d
RESULT_STATE: sacrificial_protection_proven
HINT: Metals above iron in the reactivity series lose electrons more easily, sacrificing themselves to keep iron in its reduced metallic state.
WHY: More reactive metals (Mg, Zn) have higher oxidation tendencies than iron, acting as sacrificial anodes and supplying electrons to iron (cathodic protection).

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Metals more reactive than iron (Mg, Zn) provide sacrificial protection, while less reactive metals (Cu, Pb) accelerate iron corrosion by driving iron to become the sacrificial anode.
