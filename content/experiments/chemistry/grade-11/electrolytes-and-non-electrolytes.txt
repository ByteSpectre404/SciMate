TITLE: Electrolytes and Non-Electrolytes
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electrochemistry (Unit 12)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Classify liquids and solutions into electrolytes and non-electrolytes by testing electrical conductivity using inert carbon electrodes, a DC supply, and an ammeter.

MATERIALS:
- Inert graphite carbon rods (electrodes in rubber holder)
- DC Power Supply (3.0 V - 6.0 V) & DC Ammeter / bulb
- Beakers containing: Distilled water, Acidified water (dil. H2SO4), Aqueous sodium chloride (salt solution), Coconut oil, Kerosene, Pure ethanol

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Define an electrolyte as a liquid/solution containing mobile ions capable of conducting electricity
- Distinguish strong electrolytes (acids, salts) from non-electrolytes (covalent molecular liquids like oil, kerosene, pure water, ethanol)
- Explain the role of free mobile ions vs neutral molecules in electrical conduction

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Dip the carbon electrodes into distilled water and close the circuit.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: carbon_electrodes
DROP_TARGET_ID: distilled_water_beaker
RESULT_COLOR: #94a3b8
RESULT_STATE: ammeter_zero_non_conducting
WHY: Pure distilled water consists of covalent H2O molecules with negligible auto-ionization; no free mobile ions exist to carry charge.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Dip the electrodes into aqueous sodium chloride (salt) solution.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: carbon_electrodes
DROP_TARGET_ID: salt_water_beaker
RESULT_COLOR: #3b82f6
RESULT_STATE: ammeter_deflects_strongly
WHY: Dissolved NaCl dissociates completely into free mobile Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions that migrate under electric field, conducting electric current (Electrolyte).

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Dip the electrodes into organic liquids: coconut oil, kerosene, and pure ethanol.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: carbon_electrodes
DROP_TARGET_ID: organic_liquids_beaker
RESULT_COLOR: #cbd5e1
RESULT_STATE: zero_current_non_electrolytes
WHY: Covalent organic compounds lack ionic bonds and do not form ions in liquid state, acting as electrical insulators (Non-electrolytes).

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Which of the following tested liquids acts as an electrolyte that conducts electric current?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Acidified water and sodium chloride solution (containing mobile ions)|Coconut oil and kerosene|Pure distilled water and ethanol|All covalent molecular liquids
CORRECT_OPTION: Acidified water and sodium chloride solution (containing mobile ions)
RESULT_COLOR: #1d4ed8
RESULT_STATE: electrolyte_nature_verified
HINT: Conduction of electricity in liquids requires the presence of freely moving, dissolved positive and negative ions.
WHY: Only solutions containing free-moving ions (such as dissociated acids and aqueous salts) conduct electricity; non-ionizing covalent liquids are non-electrolytes.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Liquids that contain free mobile ions (such as salt solution and acidified water) conduct electricity and are called electrolytes, whereas molecular liquids without free ions are non-electrolytes.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- Why does solid dry sodium chloride not conduct electricity, while aqueous or molten NaCl conducts readily?
- What distinguishes a strong electrolyte from a weak electrolyte?
