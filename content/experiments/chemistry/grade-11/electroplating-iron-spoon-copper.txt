TITLE: Electroplating an Iron Spoon with Copper
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electrochemistry (Unit 12)
DIFFICULTY: medium
DURATION_MINUTES: 20
DESCRIPTION: Electroplate a cleaned iron spoon with an adherent protective and decorative layer of copper metal using aqueous copper(II) sulphate and a pure copper anode.

MATERIALS:
- Cleaned iron spoon (Cathode / object to be plated)
- Pure copper plate / strip (Anode)
- Aqueous copper(II) sulphate solution (CuSO4, 0.5 M with dil. H2SO4)
- 9.0 V DC power supply with rheostat & ammeter
- Glass beaker (250 mL) & connecting wires

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Assemble an electroplating apparatus with the object at the cathode and source metal at the anode
- Understand the half-reactions: Anode dissolution (Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻) and Cathode deposition (Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu)
- Explain why the concentration of the electrolyte remains constant during electroplating with an active anode

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Connect the cleaned iron spoon to the negative terminal (Cathode) and the copper plate to the positive terminal (Anode).
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: iron_spoon_lead
DROP_TARGET_ID: dc_negative_terminal
RESULT_COLOR: #3b82f6
RESULT_STATE: electroplating_circuit_wired
WHY: In electroplating, the object to be coated must always be made the cathode (negative electrode) where metal cations gain electrons.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Immerse both the copper anode and iron spoon cathode into the copper sulphate electrolyte.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: electrodes_fixture
DROP_TARGET_ID: cuso4_bath_beaker
RESULT_COLOR: #0284c7
RESULT_STATE: immersed_plating_bath
WHY: The electrolyte contains free Cu²⁺ ions that migrate across the solution toward the cathode under the electric potential.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Pass a small, steady direct current (0.2 A) through the cell for 10 minutes.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: dc_current_regulator
DROP_TARGET_ID: cuso4_bath_beaker
RESULT_COLOR: #b45309
RESULT_STATE: uniform_copper_coating_formed
WHY: Cu²⁺ ions deposit smoothly onto the spoon (Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu), while the copper anode dissolves (Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻) to replenish the solution.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: In electroplating an object with metal, which electrode must the object to be coated always be connected to?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Cathode (negative terminal) where metal cations undergo reduction and plate out|Anode (positive terminal)|Either terminal alternately using AC current|Directly to ground without battery
CORRECT_OPTION: Cathode (negative terminal) where metal cations undergo reduction and plate out
RESULT_COLOR: #16a34a
RESULT_STATE: electroplating_rules_verified
HINT: Positively charged metal ions (cations like Cu²⁺) are attracted only to the negative electrode (cathode).
WHY: Metal cations are positively charged and migrate exclusively to the cathode (negative electrode) to gain electrons and deposit as a solid metallic layer.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: An iron spoon is electroplated with copper by connecting it as the cathode in a copper sulphate electrolyte with a pure copper anode, depositing a uniform, corrosion-resistant metallic copper coating.
