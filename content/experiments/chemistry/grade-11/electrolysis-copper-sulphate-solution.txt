TITLE: Electrolysis of Aqueous Copper(II) Sulphate Solution
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electrochemistry (Unit 12)
DIFFICULTY: medium
DURATION_MINUTES: 20
DESCRIPTION: Investigate the electrolysis of aqueous copper(II) sulphate using inert carbon electrodes, observing copper metal deposition at the cathode and oxygen gas liberation at the anode.

MATERIALS:
- Aqueous copper(II) sulphate solution (CuSO4, 0.5 M)
- Two inert graphite carbon rods
- 9.0 V DC battery with connecting leads
- Glass beaker (150 mL)
- Glowing wooden splint

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Formulate the half-reactions: Cathode reduction (Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu) and Anode oxidation (4OH⁻ → 2H2O + O2 + 4e⁻)
- Explain why the blue color of the electrolyte fades over time as Cu²⁺ ions deposit as copper metal
- Contrast inert graphite electrodes with active copper electrodes

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Connect the carbon electrodes to the 9 V battery and dip both into the blue copper sulphate solution.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: carbon_electrodes_rig
DROP_TARGET_ID: cuso4_solution_beaker
RESULT_COLOR: #0284c7
RESULT_STATE: electrolysis_underway
WHY: The external DC electric field drives Cu²⁺ and H⁺ cations toward the cathode, and SO4²⁻ and OH⁻ anions toward the anode.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Examine the negative cathode after 5 minutes of continuous current.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: cathode_rod
DROP_TARGET_ID: cuso4_solution_beaker
RESULT_COLOR: #b45309
RESULT_STATE: reddish_brown_copper_coating
WHY: Cu²⁺ ions are selectively discharged in preference to H⁺: Cu²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ → Cu(s). A shining reddish-brown layer of copper metal coats the cathode.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Examine the positive anode and test the liberated gas bubbles with a glowing splint.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: glowing_splint
DROP_TARGET_ID: anode_rod
RESULT_COLOR: #f59e0b
RESULT_STATE: oxygen_bubbles_relight_splint
WHY: OH⁻ ions are discharged in preference to SO4²⁻: 4OH⁻(aq) → 2H2O(l) + O2(g)↑ + 4e⁻. Oxygen gas relights the glowing splint.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Why does the deep blue color of the copper sulphate solution gradually fade over time during electrolysis with carbon electrodes?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Cu²⁺ ions (which cause the blue color) are removed from solution and deposited as copper metal on the cathode|The light in the room fades the dye|Sulfur crystals absorb the blue color|Water molecules decompose into ice
CORRECT_OPTION: Cu²⁺ ions (which cause the blue color) are removed from solution and deposited as copper metal on the cathode
RESULT_COLOR: #93c5fd
RESULT_STATE: color_fading_explained
HINT: The blue color is due to hydrated Cu²⁺ cations. When they gain electrons and plate out as solid Cu, their concentration decreases.
WHY: As Cu²⁺ ions are discharged onto the cathode faster than they are replenished, their concentration in solution decreases, causing the blue color to fade.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Electrolysis of aqueous copper sulphate with carbon electrodes produces a reddish-brown copper deposit on the cathode and oxygen gas at the anode, while the blue solution fades to dilute sulfuric acid.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- What would happen to the blue color if copper electrodes were used instead of inert carbon electrodes?
- What acid is left in the solution after all Cu²⁺ ions are deposited?
