TITLE: Air (Oxygen) is Essential for Rusting of Iron
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electrochemistry (Unit 12)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Demonstrate that air is essential for the corrosion of iron by comparing cleaned iron nails in ordinary air-containing water versus boiled deoxygenated water sealed with a layer of oil.

MATERIALS:
- Two boiling tubes (Tube A: ordinary water, Tube B: boiled water with oil seal)
- Cleaned iron nails (pickled in dilute HCl and rinsed)
- Coconut oil / liquid paraffin (air barrier)
- Bunsen burner & test tube rack

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Identify air (oxygen) and water as the two indispensable conditions for iron rusting
- Explain how boiling water expels dissolved gases and how an oil layer prevents re-dissolution of atmospheric air
- Describe the formation of hydrated iron(III) oxide (rust: Fe2O3·xH2O)

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Add ordinary unboiled cold tap water into Tube A and place a cleaned iron nail inside.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: iron_nail_1
DROP_TARGET_ID: tube_a_ordinary_water
RESULT_COLOR: #bae6fd
RESULT_STATE: tube_a_with_air_and_water
WHY: Ordinary tap water contains dissolved atmospheric oxygen necessary for the cathodic reduction half-reaction during corrosion.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Boil water in Tube B for 5 minutes to expel all dissolved air, add a cleaned nail, and pour a layer of coconut oil on top.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: coconut_oil_dropper
DROP_TARGET_ID: tube_b_boiled_water
RESULT_COLOR: #fef08a
RESULT_STATE: tube_b_deoxygenated_oil_sealed
WHY: Boiling drives out dissolved O2 gas; the floating oil layer acts as an impermeable barrier preventing atmospheric air from re-dissolving.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Leave both tubes undisturbed for 2 days and observe the surfaces of the iron nails.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: observation_timer
DROP_TARGET_ID: test_tube_rack
RESULT_COLOR: #991b1b
RESULT_STATE: tube_a_rusted_tube_b_shiny
WHY: In Tube A, the nail develops a thick reddish-brown flaky crust of rust (Fe2O3·xH2O); in Tube B (no oxygen), the nail remains completely bright and rust-free.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Why does the nail in Tube B (boiled water with an oil layer) not rust at all?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Boiling removed dissolved oxygen and the oil layer prevented atmospheric air from entering|The oil reacts chemically to coat the nail with zinc|Boiled water freezes iron atoms|The test tube absorbed the rust
CORRECT_OPTION: Boiling removed dissolved oxygen and the oil layer prevented atmospheric air from entering
RESULT_COLOR: #7f1d1d
RESULT_STATE: oxygen_requirement_proven
HINT: Rusting is an electrochemical oxidation requiring both water and oxygen (4Fe + 3O2 + 2xH2O → 2Fe2O3·xH2O).
WHY: Corrosion requires both water and dissolved oxygen. Without oxygen, the cathodic reaction cannot take place and rusting is completely arrested.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Iron rusts only when exposed to both moisture and air (oxygen). Excluding oxygen (by boiling water and sealing with oil) prevents iron from rusting completely.
