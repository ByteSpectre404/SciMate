TITLE: Effect of Salt (Sodium Chloride) on Rusting
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electrochemistry (Unit 12)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Compare the rusting rate of iron nails in ordinary tap water versus sodium chloride (salt) solution to explain why coastal marine environments accelerate metal corrosion.

MATERIALS:
- Two cleaned iron nails
- Two boiling tubes (Tube A: ordinary water, Tube B: 5% NaCl salt solution)
- Solid sodium chloride & distilled water
- Test tube rack

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Observe that dissolved electrolytes (NaCl) accelerate the electrochemical rusting process
- Explain how Cl⁻ and Na⁺ ions increase electrical conductivity and breakdown passive iron oxide barriers
- Discuss corrosion challenges faced in coastal towns, marine ports, and seaside infrastructure

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Add ordinary water to Tube A and 5% sodium chloride solution to Tube B.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: nacl_salt_solution
DROP_TARGET_ID: tube_b_salt
RESULT_COLOR: #bae6fd
RESULT_STATE: salt_vs_fresh_water_ready
WHY: Salt water provides a high concentration of mobile Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions, drastically increasing electrical conductivity.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Place a cleaned iron nail into each tube and leave undisturbed for 24 hours.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: iron_nails_pair
DROP_TARGET_ID: boiling_tubes_pair
RESULT_COLOR: #cbd5e1
RESULT_STATE: nails_immersed_in_media
WHY: The electrolyte allows microscopic galvanic cells across the iron surface to circulate ionic current.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Compare the extent of reddish-brown rust deposits formed in both tubes after 24 hours.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: observation_timer
DROP_TARGET_ID: test_tube_rack
RESULT_COLOR: #991b1b
RESULT_STATE: salt_tube_heavily_encrusted_rust
WHY: The nail in the salt solution exhibits intense pitting corrosion and thick rust sedimentation compared to the mild rusting in fresh water.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Why do iron structures and vehicles in coastal and marine environments rust much faster than inland?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Airborne sea salt spray increases electrolyte conductivity, accelerating electrochemical corrosion|Seaside air has twice as much oxygen|The sun is hotter near beaches|Sea breeze blows away the protective iron coating
CORRECT_OPTION: Airborne sea salt spray increases electrolyte conductivity, accelerating electrochemical corrosion
RESULT_COLOR: #7f1d1d
RESULT_STATE: marine_corrosion_effect_proven
HINT: Salt (NaCl) ions enhance the electrical conductivity of water films on the metal surface, speeding up charge transfer between anodic and cathodic micro-regions.
WHY: Dissolved salts increase the electrical conductivity of moisture droplets on metal, accelerating electron transfer and ion mobility, thereby speeding up the corrosion rate.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Dissolved sodium chloride (salt) significantly accelerates the rate of iron rusting due to enhanced electrolyte conductivity, explaining why coastal environments suffer severe metal corrosion.
