TITLE: Electrolysis of Aqueous Sodium Chloride Solution
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electrochemistry (Unit 12)
DIFFICULTY: medium
DURATION_MINUTES: 20
DESCRIPTION: Investigate the electrolysis of concentrated aqueous sodium chloride (brine) using inert carbon electrodes, identifying hydrogen gas at the cathode and chlorine gas at the anode.

MATERIALS:
- Concentrated sodium chloride solution (Brine, NaCl(aq))
- Two inert graphite carbon rods
- 9.0 V DC battery / power source with connecting wires
- Glass beaker & red litmus paper

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Define electrolysis as the decomposition of an electrolyte by passing a direct electric current
- Predict electrode reactions: Cathode reduction (2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H2) and Anode oxidation (2Cl⁻ → Cl2 + 2e⁻)
- Understand the industrial Chlor-Alkali process for manufacturing NaOH, Cl2, and H2

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Connect two carbon rod electrodes to the 9 V DC battery terminals and immerse into the NaCl solution.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: carbon_electrodes_rig
DROP_TARGET_ID: nacl_brine_beaker
RESULT_COLOR: #3b82f6
RESULT_STATE: active_gas_evolution_both_electrodes
WHY: DC voltage drives selective discharge of ions: H⁺ is reduced faster than Na⁺, and Cl⁻ is oxidized.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Observe gas bubbling at the negative cathode (connected to the negative battery terminal).
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: cathode_probe
DROP_TARGET_ID: nacl_brine_beaker
RESULT_COLOR: #f8fafc
RESULT_STATE: cathode_hydrogen_gas_bubbles
WHY: Cathode reaction: 2H⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ → H2(g)↑. Colorless odorless hydrogen gas bubbles off rapidly.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Observe gas bubbling at the positive anode (connected to positive battery terminal) and test with moist red litmus.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: moist_red_litmus
DROP_TARGET_ID: anode_probe
RESULT_COLOR: #fef08a
RESULT_STATE: anode_chlorine_bleaches_litmus_white
WHY: Anode reaction: 2Cl⁻(aq) → Cl2(g)↑ + 2e⁻. Pale yellow-green chlorine gas evolves with a bleaching and choking odor.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: What happens to the remaining solution in the beaker as H⁺ and Cl⁻ are discharged as gases?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: It becomes a basic solution of sodium hydroxide (NaOH) with accumulated Na⁺ and OH⁻ ions|It becomes pure concentrated sulfuric acid|It turns into solid sodium metal|It evaporates completely into vacuum
CORRECT_OPTION: It becomes a basic solution of sodium hydroxide (NaOH) with accumulated Na⁺ and OH⁻ ions
RESULT_COLOR: #16a34a
RESULT_STATE: chlor_alkali_process_proven
HINT: Water dissociates into H⁺ and OH⁻. As H⁺ and Cl⁻ evolve as gases, Na⁺ and OH⁻ remain in the liquid.
WHY: Removal of H⁺ and Cl⁻ leaves Na⁺ and OH⁻ ions in solution, producing aqueous sodium hydroxide (the Chlor-Alkali industrial process).

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Electrolysis of aqueous sodium chloride produces hydrogen gas at the cathode, chlorine gas at the anode, and leaves a solution of sodium hydroxide (NaOH).
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- Why are H⁺ ions reduced at the cathode in preference to Na⁺ ions?
- What are three important industrial uses of the products (NaOH, Cl2, H2) obtained from brine electrolysis?
