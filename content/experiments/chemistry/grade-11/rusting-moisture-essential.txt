TITLE: Moisture (Water) is Essential for Rusting of Iron
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electrochemistry (Unit 12)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Demonstrate that moisture (water) is an indispensable requirement for the rusting of iron by comparing iron nails inside dry air containing anhydrous calcium chloride versus normal humid air.

MATERIALS:
- Four cleaned iron nails (two pushed through rubber stopper A, two through rubber stopper B)
- Tube A: Empty boiling tube (containing normal atmospheric moisture)
- Tube B: Boiling tube containing anhydrous calcium chloride (CaCl2 drying agent)
- Test tube rack

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Prove that dry air alone cannot rust iron without water
- Understand the role of water as an electrolyte medium for ion transport during corrosion
- Explain how desiccants and drying agents protect sensitive metallic instruments from corrosion

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Fit Stopper A (with 2 clean iron nails piercing through) onto the empty boiling tube containing normal air.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: stopper_a_nails
DROP_TARGET_ID: tube_a_humid_air
RESULT_COLOR: #cbd5e1
RESULT_STATE: humid_air_setup
WHY: Normal laboratory air contains sufficient ambient water vapor (relative humidity) to support electrochemical corrosion cells.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Add anhydrous calcium chloride drying pellets into Tube B and seal it with Stopper B (with 2 clean nails).
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: stopper_b_nails
DROP_TARGET_ID: tube_b_desiccant
RESULT_COLOR: #f8fafc
RESULT_STATE: dry_air_desiccant_setup
WHY: Anhydrous CaCl2 is strongly hygroscopic and absorbs all water vapor inside Tube B, creating completely dry air around the inside nail portions.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Leave both tubes undisturbed for 3 days and compare the nails inside versus outside each tube.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: observation_timer
DROP_TARGET_ID: test_tube_rack
RESULT_COLOR: #991b1b
RESULT_STATE: tube_a_nails_rusted_tube_b_inside_shiny
WHY: In Tube A, the nails rust both inside and outside. In Tube B, the nail sections outside rust, but the sections inside the dry tube remain completely clean and shiny.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Why do the nail segments inside Tube B (with anhydrous calcium chloride) show zero rusting?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Anhydrous CaCl2 absorbed all moisture; rusting cannot occur in dry air without water|Calcium chloride gas coated the nails with plastic|The nails inside Tube B lost their iron properties|Air pressure inside Tube B dropped to zero
CORRECT_OPTION: Anhydrous CaCl2 absorbed all moisture; rusting cannot occur in dry air without water
RESULT_COLOR: #7f1d1d
RESULT_STATE: moisture_requirement_verified
HINT: Water is needed as the liquid electrolyte to facilitate the transfer of Fe²⁺ and OH⁻ ions.
WHY: Rusting is an electrochemical process that requires moisture (water) as an electrolyte medium. Without moisture, iron cannot corrode even in the presence of oxygen.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Moisture (water) is strictly essential for the corrosion of iron. In perfectly dry air (achieved using anhydrous CaCl2), iron remains pristine and does not rust.
