TITLE: Component of Air Essential for Rusting (Oxygen Fraction)
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electrochemistry (Unit 12)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Determine which fractional component of air is consumed during rusting by inverting a test tube containing moist iron wool over a water basin and measuring the 1/5th volume rise.

MATERIALS:
- Moist steel iron wool lump
- Two identical boiling tubes (Tube 1: experimental with iron wool, Tube 2: empty control tube)
- Water basin / trough containing water
- Retort stand with dual clamps
- Metric ruler

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Demonstrate that iron rusting specifically consumes the oxygen fraction of atmospheric air
- Measure the approximately 20% (1/5th) liquid column rise corresponding to atmospheric O2 percentage
- Understand that remaining atmospheric nitrogen (~78%) does not support or participate in rusting

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Push a moist lump of steel iron wool into the bottom of Tube 1 and invert it in the water basin.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: moist_iron_wool_tube
DROP_TARGET_ID: water_basin
RESULT_COLOR: #bae6fd
RESULT_STATE: inverted_iron_wool_tube_primed
WHY: The moist iron wool exposes high surface area of iron and moisture to the trapped column of atmospheric air.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Invert an empty boiling tube (Tube 2) beside it in the same water basin as an experimental control.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: empty_control_tube
DROP_TARGET_ID: water_basin
RESULT_COLOR: #bae6fd
RESULT_STATE: control_tube_primed
WHY: The control tube verifies that water level changes are not caused merely by room temperature or atmospheric pressure fluctuations.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Leave the apparatus undisturbed for 3 days and measure the water level rise inside both tubes.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: metric_ruler
DROP_TARGET_ID: water_basin
RESULT_COLOR: #991b1b
RESULT_STATE: water_level_rises_by_one_fifth
WHY: As iron reacts with oxygen to form rust, O2 is removed from the gas volume, creating a partial vacuum that draws water up by exactly ~1/5th (20%) of the tube volume.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Why does the water level inside the tube containing iron wool rise by approximately one-fifth (20%) of the total air volume?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Oxygen gas (which constitutes about 21% of atmospheric air) is completely consumed during rusting|Water evaporates into nitrogen gas|Iron wool sucks water by capillary suction|The glass tube expands by 20%
CORRECT_OPTION: Oxygen gas (which constitutes about 21% of atmospheric air) is completely consumed during rusting
RESULT_COLOR: #7f1d1d
RESULT_STATE: oxygen_fraction_verified
HINT: Air contains ~78% Nitrogen, ~21% Oxygen, and ~1% Argon. Only oxygen reacts with iron during corrosion.
WHY: Rusting selectively consumes oxygen from the enclosed air. Because oxygen accounts for ~1/5th (21%) of air by volume, atmospheric pressure forces water up to replace the consumed gas.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Iron rusting selectively uses up the oxygen component of atmospheric air (~20% of air volume), proving that oxygen is the specific gaseous reactant consumed during corrosion.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- What gas remains inside the test tube after the water level stops rising?
- If pure oxygen were used in the tube instead of air, what would happen to the water level?
