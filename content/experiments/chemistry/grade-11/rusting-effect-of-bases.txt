TITLE: Effect of Bases (Alkalinity) on Rate of Rusting
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electrochemistry (Unit 12)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Compare the rusting of iron nails in neutral water versus alkaline sodium hydroxide (NaOH) solution to demonstrate that basic solutions retard and inhibit iron corrosion.

MATERIALS:
- Two cleaned iron nails
- Two boiling tubes (Tube A: ordinary water, Tube B: dilute sodium hydroxide NaOH solution)
- Sodium hydroxide pellets & distilled water
- Test tube rack

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Observe that alkaline (basic) solutions significantly retard or prevent the corrosion of iron
- Understand that high OH⁻ ion concentration promotes the formation of a stable, passive protective iron oxide/hydroxide barrier
- Explain why rebar in concrete (calcium hydroxide alkaline environment) remains protected from corrosion for decades

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Add ordinary water to Tube A and 1.0 M sodium hydroxide (NaOH) solution to Tube B.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: naoh_solution_bottle
DROP_TARGET_ID: tube_b_base
RESULT_COLOR: #f0fdf4
RESULT_STATE: alkaline_vs_neutral_medium_ready
WHY: NaOH solution provides a high concentration of basic hydroxide ions (OH⁻) with a high pH (~13).

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Place a cleaned iron nail into each tube and leave undisturbed for 48 hours.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: iron_nails_pair
DROP_TARGET_ID: boiling_tubes_pair
RESULT_COLOR: #cbd5e1
RESULT_STATE: nails_immersed_in_base_and_water
WHY: Contrasting neutral vs alkaline conditions reveals the influence of OH⁻ concentration on electrochemical passivation.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Examine both nails after 48 hours.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: observation_timer
DROP_TARGET_ID: test_tube_rack
RESULT_COLOR: #3b82f6
RESULT_STATE: nail_in_base_completely_rust_free
WHY: The nail in ordinary water has developed noticeable brown rust, while the nail in NaOH solution remains completely shiny and rust-free.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: How does an alkaline (basic) solution like sodium hydroxide affect the rate of rusting of iron?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Basic solutions inhibit and drastically decrease (slow down or prevent) the rate of rusting|Basic solutions cause iron to dissolve into gas instantly|Basic solutions double the rust formation|Bases have zero effect on iron
CORRECT_OPTION: Basic solutions inhibit and drastically decrease (slow down or prevent) the rate of rusting
RESULT_COLOR: #1d4ed8
RESULT_STATE: alkaline_passivation_proven
HINT: In high pH environments, iron forms an insoluble, microscopic passive film that shields the underlying metal.
WHY: Alkaline conditions stabilize an insoluble, tightly adhering passive oxide film on the iron surface, preventing Fe²⁺ dissolution and dramatically retarding corrosion.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Alkaline (basic) solutions like sodium hydroxide retard and inhibit the rusting of iron, explaining why steel reinforcement bars are naturally preserved inside alkaline concrete.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- Why does reinforced concrete protect embedded steel rebar from rusting unless carbonation (CO2 exposure) neutralizes the alkalinity?
- Contrast the effects of acids (accelerate rusting) and bases (retard rusting) on iron.
