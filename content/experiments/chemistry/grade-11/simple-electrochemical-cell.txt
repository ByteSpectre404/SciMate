TITLE: Simple Electrochemical Voltaic Cell
SUBJECT: chemistry
GRADE: 11
UNIT: Electrochemistry (Unit 12)
DIFFICULTY: medium
DURATION_MINUTES: 20
DESCRIPTION: Construct a simple primary electrochemical cell (Voltaic cell) using zinc and copper electrodes in dilute sulfuric acid to generate an electric current through an external circuit.

MATERIALS:
- Clean zinc strip (Anode / negative electrode)
- Clean copper strip (Cathode / positive electrode)
- Glass beaker with dilute sulfuric acid (H2SO4, 1.0 M)
- Sensitive DC ammeter / galvanometer (-30 to +30 mA)
- Connecting wires with alligator clips

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Assemble a simple voltaic cell converting chemical energy directly into electrical energy
- Identify the negative electrode/anode (Zinc oxidation: Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻) and positive electrode/cathode (Copper reduction: 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H2)
- Trace external electron flow from zinc (anode) through the ammeter to copper (cathode)

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Connect the zinc strip and copper strip to the terminals of the DC ammeter with insulated wires.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: electrode_wires_assembly
DROP_TARGET_ID: ammeter
RESULT_COLOR: #3b82f6
RESULT_STATE: external_circuit_wired
WHY: The external conducting wire provides a path for electrons to travel from the oxidation site to the reduction site.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Immerse both metal strips simultaneously into the beaker of dilute sulfuric acid without touching each other.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: electrode_wires_assembly
DROP_TARGET_ID: acid_beaker
RESULT_COLOR: #22c55e
RESULT_STATE: ammeter_deflects_bubbles_on_copper
WHY: Zinc oxidizes (Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻); electrons flow through the ammeter to copper, where H⁺ ions reduce to H2 gas bubbles on the copper strip.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Disconnect the wire from the ammeter while keeping electrodes submerged.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: ammeter_wire
DROP_TARGET_ID: disconnect_terminal
RESULT_COLOR: #94a3b8
RESULT_STATE: ammeter_drops_to_zero
WHY: Breaking the external circuit prevents electron transit, stopping the continuous galvanic current.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: At which electrode do hydrogen gas bubbles predominantly evolve when the circuit is connected through the ammeter?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: At the copper cathode (positive electrode)|At the zinc anode only|Equally inside the glass walls|No gas evolves in an electrochemical cell
CORRECT_OPTION: At the copper cathode (positive electrode)
RESULT_COLOR: #16a34a
RESULT_STATE: galvanic_mechanism_verified
HINT: Electrons released by zinc oxidation travel through the external wire to the copper strip, where H⁺ ions accept them.
WHY: Electrons stream through the external circuit to the copper electrode, where aqueous H⁺ ions gain electrons (2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H2↑), forming bubbles on the copper surface.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: In a simple electrochemical cell, zinc is oxidized at the anode and hydrogen ions are reduced at the copper cathode, establishing a continuous flow of electrons through the external circuit.
