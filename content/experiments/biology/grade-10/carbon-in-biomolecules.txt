TITLE: Identification of Carbon (C) in Biomolecules
SUBJECT: biology
GRADE: 10
UNIT: Biomolecules
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Identify the universal presence of elemental carbon in biological tissues including spinach stems, fish, and chickpeas.

MATERIALS:
- Spinach stems
- Piece of fish
- Chickpea
- Crucible
- White paper
- Heat source

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Prove that carbon is the primary structural backbone of all organic biomolecules
- Observe the formation of charred elemental carbon residue upon heating

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Place crushed spinach stem pulp into a crucible.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: spinach_pulp
DROP_TARGET_ID: crucible
RESULT_COLOR: #16a34a
RESULT_STATE: moist_spinach_pulp
WHY: Spinach stems contain carbohydrates, proteins, and lipids rich in carbon chains.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Heat the sample strongly with a burner until only dry residue remains.
ACTION_TYPE: toggle_switch
DRAG_ITEM_ID: heat_source
DROP_TARGET_ID: crucible
RESULT_COLOR: #09090b
RESULT_STATE: charred_black_carbon
WHY: Strong heat burns off volatile elements (H, O, N) leaving elemental carbon char behind.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Rub the final dark residue against a piece of white paper.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: carbon_residue
DROP_TARGET_ID: white_paper
RESULT_COLOR: #09090b
RESULT_STATE: charcoal_streak
WHY: Rubbing tests the texture and color marking of the charred material.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: What mark is left on the white paper by the charred residue?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: A black mark (charcoal)|A bright yellow streak|A clear liquid stain|No mark at all
CORRECT_OPTION: A black mark (charcoal)
RESULT_COLOR: #09090b
RESULT_STATE: charcoal_streak
HINT: Carbon produces a charcoal black mark on paper.
WHY: The black mark is elemental carbon, proving biomolecules contain carbon backbones.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Heating biological samples leaves a black residue that marks paper like charcoal, confirming carbon is present in living tissues.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- Why do all organic biomolecules leave black carbon residue when charred?
- What gases are released during the initial heating phase?
