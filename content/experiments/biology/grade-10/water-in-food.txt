TITLE: Identification of Water as a Constituent in Food
SUBJECT: biology
GRADE: 10
UNIT: Biomolecules
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Identify the presence of water in organic food materials such as dried meat, egg shell, and plant leaves through gentle heating.

MATERIALS:
- Dried meat sample
- Dried egg shell sample
- Dried plant leaves sample
- Crucible
- Glass sheet
- Anhydrous cobalt chloride paper
- Heat source (Bunsen burner)

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Confirm that food materials contain water as an essential constituent
- Use anhydrous cobalt chloride paper (blue to pink) to test for moisture

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Place ground dried plant leaves into the crucible and set it on the heat source.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: plant_leaves_sample
DROP_TARGET_ID: crucible
RESULT_COLOR: #16a34a
RESULT_STATE: heating_food_sample
WHY: Heating breaks down hydrogen bonds and evaporates trapped water vapor from food cells.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Hold a glass sheet above the heating crucible to collect condensation.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: glass_sheet
DROP_TARGET_ID: crucible
RESULT_COLOR: #e0f2fe
RESULT_STATE: condensed_water_droplets
WHY: The cool glass sheet condenses hot water vapor rising from the heated sample into droplets.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Test the collected droplets on the glass sheet using blue anhydrous cobalt chloride paper.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: cobalt_chloride_paper
DROP_TARGET_ID: glass_sheet
RESULT_COLOR: #ec4899
RESULT_STATE: cobalt_chloride_pink
WHY: Anhydrous cobalt chloride is a chemical indicator specific for water.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: What color change occurs on the anhydrous cobalt chloride paper when it touches the droplets?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Changes from blue to pink|Changes from white to blue|Turns dark black|No color change
CORRECT_OPTION: Changes from blue to pink
RESULT_COLOR: #ec4899
RESULT_STATE: cobalt_chloride_pink
HINT: Blue cobalt chloride turns pink when hydrated by water.
WHY: Water reacts with anhydrous cobalt chloride (blue) converting it to hydrated cobalt chloride (pink), confirming water is present.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Droplets condensed on the glass sheet turned blue cobalt chloride paper to pink, confirming that water is a constituent of biomolecules in food.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- Why does anhydrous copper sulphate turn from white to blue in the presence of water?
- Why is water essential in living cells even in dry food parts?
