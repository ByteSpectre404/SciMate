TITLE: Observing Mucor on Bread
SUBJECT: biology
GRADE: 10
UNIT: The World of Life (Unit 13)
DIFFICULTY: easy
DURATION_MINUTES: 15
DESCRIPTION: Cultivate and observe the microscopic structures of the fungus Mucor (bread mold), including mycelium, hyphae, and sporangia under a compound light microscope.

MATERIALS:
- Slice of bread
- Water dropper
- Glass slide with coverslip
- Inoculation needle / glass rod
- Compound light microscope

LEARNING_OUTCOMES:
- Identify fungal hyphae, mycelium, and sporangia of Mucor under magnification
- Understand fungal spore germination on moist carbohydrate-rich substrates
- Differentiate between unicellular and multicellular eukaryotic micro-organisms

===STEP===
ID: step-1
INSTRUCTION: Add a few drops of water onto a slice of bread and leave in a dark warm place for 2 days.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: water_dropper
DROP_TARGET_ID: bread_slice
RESULT_COLOR: #fef3c7
RESULT_STATE: moist_bread_mold_mycelium
WHY: Fungal spores present in the air require moisture and carbohydrate nutrients to germinate into white cottony mycelium.

===STEP===
ID: step-2
INSTRUCTION: Use a needle/glass rod to collect a small tuft of Mucor mycelium and transfer onto a glass slide.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: glass_rod
DROP_TARGET_ID: glass_slide
RESULT_COLOR: #f8fafc
RESULT_STATE: fungal_specimen_slide
WHY: Mounting a thin specimen prevents overlapping and enables light to pass through the specimen.

===STEP===
ID: step-3
INSTRUCTION: Place a cover slip gently over the specimen drop to prepare the wet mount.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: cover_slip
DROP_TARGET_ID: glass_slide
RESULT_COLOR: #f1f5f9
RESULT_STATE: prepared_mucor_slide
WHY: Lowering the coverslip at an angle avoids trapping air bubbles that obstruct microscopic viewing.

===STEP===
ID: step-4
INSTRUCTION: Place the prepared slide on the microscope stage and observe under low and medium power.
ACTION_TYPE: drag_place
DRAG_ITEM_ID: prepared_mucor_slide
DROP_TARGET_ID: microscope_stage
RESULT_COLOR: #ecfdf5
RESULT_STATE: mucor_hyphae_and_sporangia
WHY: Light passes through the fungal hyphae, magnifying the tubular non-septate hyphae and globose black sporangia.

===STEP===
ID: step-5
INSTRUCTION: What is the spherical dark structure at the tip of an aerial hypha in Mucor called?
ACTION_TYPE: select_observation
OPTIONS: Sporangium (containing spores)|Chloroplast|Root hair|Pollen sac
CORRECT_OPTION: Sporangium (containing spores)
RESULT_COLOR: #15803d
RESULT_STATE: identified_sporangium
HINT: It is a spore-producing capsule that bursts when mature to disperse airborne spores.
WHY: Mucor reproduces asexually by producing numerous haploid spores inside terminal spherical sporangia.

===OUTCOME===
SUMMARY: Mucor grows as a network of branching hyphae called a mycelium. It reproduces asexually by forming sporangia that release microscopic spores.
REFLECTION_QUESTIONS:
- Why does Mucor grow rapidly on moist bread kept in dark, warm conditions?
- Are the hyphae of Mucor septate or non-septate (coenocytic)?
